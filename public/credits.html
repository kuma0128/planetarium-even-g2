Data, calculations & licenses

G2 Planetarium calculates sky-object positions locally from your observing location and selected time. The catalogs and calculation data are bundled with the app.

  • Stars: David Nash / HYG Database v4.1. This app extracts 2,865 stars through magnitude 5.5, using their J2000 coordinates, proper motions, magnitudes, and names. The derived catalog remains licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0. License and attribution.
  • Constellation lines and standard names: Olaf Frohn / D3-Celestial. Contains 89 figures, with Serpens split into two parts. BSD 3-Clause.
  • Astronomy calculations: Don Cross / Astronomy Engine 2.1.19. Includes precession, nutation, lunar and planetary parallax, and normal atmospheric refraction. MIT.
  • Magnetic correction: Natural Atlas / geomagnetism 0.2.0 and the NOAA World Magnetic Model. Declination uses today's physical date, independent of the simulated sky time. Apache 2.0.
  • G2 integration: Even Hub SDK 0.0.15. MIT.
  • App source code: G2 Planetarium, licensed under GPL-3.0-only. Catalogs and libraries retain their separate licenses above.

Understanding the display

Buildings, weather, terrain, and light pollution are not modeled. The Sun and Moon are symbols sized for visibility; their apparent diameters and the Moon's phase shape are not reproduced. This is an enlarged sky map rather than an optically calibrated AR overlay.

For phone compass mode, hold the phone flat and point its physical top edge in your viewing direction. The app does not track the orientation of your head or glasses. Magnetic north is the default input reference. Select True north if your phone or external compass already applies magnetic correction.

Magnetic correction is unavailable outside the model's supported dates or where the horizontal magnetic field is too weak. In those cases, use a true-north compass and select True north in the app.
